03 — The Data Model

PROV-O Primitives, Entity Schemas, and the Accountability Invariant

Standardizing actors, activities, and artifacts directly on W3C PROV-O ontology terms.

All research interactions in this platform map to standard W3C PROV-O classes: Activity (threads and decisions), Agent (human researchers and software models), Entity (messages, code, figures, and datasets), and Bundle (export packages). This vocabulary eliminates naming ambiguities and guarantees semantic interoperability.

1. Thread (`prov:Activity`)

A Thread is a bounded, time-ordered research activity—such as an experimental benchmark, algorithm design debate, or manuscript drafting milestone. It generalizes the existing ThreadEntry ledger record.

2. Agent (`prov:Agent`)

An Agent represents an actor within the system, divided into two explicit subclasses:

  • prov:Person: A verified human researcher, associated with an ORCID, organizational affiliation (prov:Organization), and platform aliases.
  • prov:SoftwareAgent: An AI model, bot, or automated script (e.g., gpt-4o-2026-05-15 or local Ollama instances) operating under human supervision.

Open question, flagged in external review: not every contributor — students and new researchers especially — will hold an ORCID at the time of their contribution. The schema currently treats onm:orcid as the binding identifier for prov:Person; a durable fallback identifier for pre-ORCID contributors has not yet been defined.

3. Message (`prov:Entity`)

A Message is the atomic communicative act within a thread. It carries content, timestamp, source platform permalinks, and cryptographic checksums. Invariant: Every Message MUST have a human prov:Person author. No communicative event enters the permanent graph without a human taking responsibility for its submission.

Open question, flagged in external review: Discord and GitHub both already carry non-trivial bot/CI/Q&A-bot traffic, and this invariant does not yet specify what happens to bot-originated content — whether it is dropped, attributed to whoever operates the bot, or modeled instead as a prov:SoftwareAgent-authored Entity rather than a Message. This needs an explicit ingestion rule before the invariant can be considered fully specified.

4. Artifact (`prov:Entity`) & The Accountability Invariant

An Artifact is a derived scientific deliverable: code diffs, figures, benchmark datasets, or manuscript sections. To resolve journal policies on generative AI, Artifacts enforce a two-tiered attribution pattern:

Attribution FieldPermitted Agent TypeSemantic Definition
accountable_author prov:Person (Human Only) The researcher ethically and scientifically accountable for introducing and verifying the artifact.
originator prov:Person OR prov:SoftwareAgent The physical generator of the content (human intellect or generative AI model).

5. Decision (`prov:Activity`)

A Decision records a structured consensus milestone within a thread: the formal proposition, voting members, quorum threshold, and selected outcome.

Quorum eligibility, vote weighting, and support for alternative mechanisms (e.g., a private ballot rather than a public reaction) are not yet specified — see 05 — Open Questions. The system is designed to record and surface these signals for a community's own governance process, not to dictate what counts as consensus; it is a resource for governance, not the governor.

6. Context Bundle & Provenance Record (`prov:Bundle`)

A Bundle is a self-describing research package containing a subset of the graph, rendered as Markdown, PDF, or RO-Crate JSON-LD for external citation.

7. Editorial Gate / Publication Authorization — Open Question

Thread, Agent, Message, Artifact, Decision, and Bundle are all modeled above as explicit primitives with a full audit trail. The Editorial Gate approval that authorizes a Bundle to be packaged and deposited publicly is not currently modeled as a first-class primitive — it exists procedurally (a human review queue) but not as a graph entity of its own. This means "who signed off on making this public, and when" does not yet carry the same rigor as everything else in the graph. Flagged in external review; not yet designed. One candidate: model publication authorization as its own qualified Decision or Activity, following the same prov:qualifiedAssociation pattern used for artifact accountability (see 04 — Standards Alignment).
