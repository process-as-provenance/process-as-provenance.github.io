05 — Open Questions & Roadmap

Co-Design Session Agenda and Phased Implementation Plan

Structured operational decisions to finalize between the Research Lead, Infrastructure Lead, and Partner Consortia.

This document frames the concrete co-design agenda for our working session, structured across several policy groups, followed by a phased engineering roadmap. Groups A–D represent core governance; Groups E–G integrate critical insights from domain experts regarding publication scoping, federated feeds, and unbundled academic publishing.

Group A: Contribution Metrics & CRediT Tagging

Decision Item 1

Mechanisms for Scoring Effort: Raw event volume (Discord messages, GitHub commits) biases credit toward chattiness over architectural insight. We propose qualitative CRediT role tagging during milestone reviews, grounded by the event ledger. Does the Research Lead approve milestone role tagging as the primary attribution signal?

Decision Item 1b

Dispute Resolution for Contested Tags: Milestone tagging relocates the credit question rather than resolving it outright — it replaces "who posted the most" with "who ran the review, or who was in the room." Direction under consideration: role tags may be AI-assisted, drafted from the activity log as a starting suggestion, but always require human review before being recorded. For Open Neuromorphic specifically, the intent is to keep this as permissionless as possible, with contested tags resolved by community vote rather than a single reviewer's call. The exact appeals mechanism is an open design topic.

Group B: Privacy, Redaction, & Email Ingestion

Decision Item 2

Consent Posture for Email: Ingesting personal mailboxes creates privacy risks for untracked third parties. We propose Mode 1 (explicit CC to archive@project.org) with automated sender scrubbing. Is this posture sufficient for lab workflows?

Decision Item 2b

Pre-Publication Review as the Primary Safeguard: Before any bundle is packaged into an RO-Crate and deposited to Zenodo, the intent is that its exact drafted contents are circulated for human review and sign-off. This is the main defense against private material becoming a permanent, citable, DOI'd record — it happens while the bundle is still fully editable, before checksums are applied.

Decision Item 2c — Unresolved

Post-Publication Redaction: Pre-publication review doesn't cover everything — a privacy problem may only be discovered after a DOI has already been minted and the bundle checksummed. SHA-256 integrity guarantees and Zenodo's expectation of DOI permanence pull in the opposite direction from any promise to redact after the fact. A superseding, re-versioned crate with a new DOI and a tombstone notice on the original is our primary candidate.

Group C: Publication Scopes & The "Why a DOI?" Policy

Decision Item 3a

Configurable Default Routing Scopes: How should the default publication posture be set for a given workspace? For instance, an open community channel (like Open Neuromorphic Discord) may default to Tier 2 (Federated) or Tier 4 (Public) to support radical open science, while an experimental university testbed (like Stanford bench workflows) may default to Tier 1 (Internal Lab RAG) until formal milestone sign-off. Does the team agree that routing defaults should be workspace-configurable rather than globally hardcoded?

Decision Item 3b

Federated Syndication via CList: Stephen Downes demonstrated that personal learning environments like CList can ingest RSS/JSON-LD streams to aggregate cross-lab discussions across institutions. Should Phase 2 prioritize a bi-directional CList adapter over a centralized web dashboard to connect Stanford, Strathclyde, and ONM?

Decision Item 3c

Funder Matchmaking & Grant Compliance: Federal funding bodies (NSF POSE, NIH BRAIN) and philanthropies (Sloan, Schmidt) spend millions seeking visibility into non-paper R&D outputs. Should PaP offer an opt-in "Tier 3: Funder Horizon" feed where labs publish scrubbed, high-level capability digests to attract funding directly?

Group D: Pilot Selection & Deployment Scope

Decision Item 4

Phase 1 Deployment Scope: Selecting active Stanford neuroscience benchmark workstreams (Sébastien Proulx), the University of Strathclyde NSSP Lab (Gaetano Di Caterina), and the Open Neuromorphic community working group to run pilot pipelines and validate empirical workflows.

Group E: Decision & Voting Mechanisms

Decision Item 5 — Unresolved

Quorum Eligibility & Weighting: Converting reactions into structured Decision records is an improvement, but treating every reaction equally is not — an unscoped quorum (any Discord member's emoji) risks formalizing channel popularity into a citable scientific record rather than informed consensus. Voter eligibility (e.g., named contributors on the relevant thread) and weighting are not yet specified.

Decision Item 6 — Unresolved

Alternative Voting Mechanisms: Public reactions aren't the only way to register a vote, and won't always be appropriate — a private or silent ballot (e.g., submitted by direct message) may be needed for sensitive decisions. Which mechanisms to support, and when, is a to-do rather than a settled design.

Group F: Security & Trust

Decision Item 7 — Unresolved

LLM Context-Bundle Threat Model: Context bundles fed to drafting LLMs are assembled automatically from Discord, GitHub, and document content — including, in ONM's case, an open public community. That is a prompt-injection and data-poisoning surface, not only a privacy one: adversarial content in an ingested channel could shape what a grounded LLM writes into a manuscript, which then re-enters the same provenance graph as an originator-attributed artifact. Currently only PII/secret scrubbing is planned. A security threat model for this pathway is needed before Module 1 is wired into an open community.

Group G: Flagged in External Review, Not Yet Discussed

Open Question

ORCID Fallback: The Agent schema assumes an ORCID binding for prov:Person. Not every contributor — students and new researchers especially — will have one at the time of contribution. What is the fallback identifier?

Open Question

Unbundling Science vs. Impact Factor Pressures: As Paul Kirkland highlighted, academic institutions prioritize Q1 publications for tenure and promotion. How does ONM ensure that promoting unbundled RO-Crates and negative-result compendiums complements rather than competes with researchers' need to publish in high-impact narrative venues?
