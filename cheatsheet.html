This walkthrough follows a single, real lab event — flagging sub‑042's motion artifact in Discord — as it physically turns into structured database rows, an LLM-derived Methods section, and a citable, unbundled research package.

Why you don't need a DOI for everything: Not every casual Discord discussion needs an immutable Zenodo DOI minted immediately. Public DOIs are reserved for verified milestones, unbundling the narrative paper from its reproducible computational engine.

Part 2 — Vocabulary

Four standards, translated into lab terms

You will only meet four vocabularies in this project. Here is each one, explained in plain language.

W3C Recommendation

W3C PROV-O

What it is: A universal grammar of three core nouns — who (Agent), what happened (Activity), and what was created or used (Entity).

In your lab: "Elena ran the QA script on sub-042" becomes Agent=Elena, Activity=ScriptRun, Entity=cohort_euler_metrics.csv.
ANSI/NISO Standard

ANSI/NISO CRediT

What it is: A fixed taxonomy of 14 specific contributor roles (Investigation, Software, Methodology, Writing) so scientific credit is based on verified work rather than author-order negotiations.

In your lab: Elena's QA script run gets tagged credit:software; your prompt-and-review of Section 2.3 gets credit:writing-review-editing.
Academic Ontology

PAV Ontology

What it is: Two labels that strictly distinguish accountable human authors from generative software tools, satisfying Nature and bioRxiv AI disclosure rules.

In your lab: You are always pav:authoredBy (Human Accountable). Claude is pav:createdWith (Software Originator).
Packaging Standard

RO-Crate 1.1

What it is: A standardized specification for packaging mixed lab files (code, data, transcripts, PDFs) into a self-describing zip with a JSON-LD packing slip.

In your lab: The Euler metrics CSV, decision log, QA code, and final Section 2.3 are packaged with ro-crate-metadata.json for Zenodo DOI minting.
